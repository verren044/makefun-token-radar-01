How the implied value is priced

One function scores every row, from the snapshot’s own figures — never from a hand-kept list of names. A protocol’s implied value is its TVL times the multiple the market already pays for its category, adjusted for growth, maturity, chain breadth and youth.

Age is the time since the protocol was first listed, the best record of how long it has run with no token; an unlisted age uses 365 days. A protocol tracked under 90 days takes a 0.85x youth discount. Multiples are clamped to [0.02, 2]; a category needs 4 live protocols of its own, else it takes the global median of 0.169x.

Candidate configurations the walk-forward test compared

Six ways to price the same protocols, each calibrated on the training folds only and scored on protocols it never saw. Mean held-out Spearman is the ranking test; the ratio column is the market-cap-to-TVL ratio test, and MdAPE is the median absolute error on market cap. The ranking is carried by TVL scale: even TVL times one global median ranks held-out market caps at 0.23. The ratio is not predictable from the multiple models — they score near zero on it — but the fitted log-regression is the first candidate that ranks the ratio (0.357), while losing the ranking test itself.

Fitted launch pricing (radar-4)

The fitted launch price is an ordinary least-squares fit of log market cap on log TVL, log age, chain count and seven-day change, over the 128 live protocols with a positive market cap, plus a per-category residual effect shrunk toward zero by n/(n+4). R² 0.216 — the features explain about a fifth of the log market cap — so the fitted price is a weak second estimate, and the board never ranks by it.
